# Plural FAQ

## Is this a joke, roleplay, etc?

No.

## Can I ask you a question?

Of course. If you've got this far into our profile repo you probably have a good idea of what will be a good question and what you can just research. We enjoy engaging with people about plurality overall.

## How do I refer to you / systems in general?

For us, you can refer to us like any singlet. Our pronouns are she/they collectively, unless you are speaking about a specific member who has different pronouns. For systems in general, listen to them. If a profile states pronouns, those usually refer to the system as a whole, but if a specific member introduces themselves, shares a different pronoun set, etc, then use *their* pronouns when talking about them. The extent to which this will vary also varies by system.

We (and many other systems) disclose our specific front state and details of members in some spaces, but not all - it is generally considered impolite to break this boundary if it exists by bringing specific member mentions into spaces where a system does not disclose them, for example if you know that system in spaces of both types.

## Does this affect your code, how I should work with you, etc?

For us, no. We have a single pool of memory and generally operate with a high degree of cooperation. We are load-balanced. We have slight differences in *mentality* around some technical aspects, but these have not resulted in actual technical conflict, perhaps at most a quick followup "actually, changed this to a better way, also found and fixed another bug" type commit. If you've worked with an engineer with ADHD, you can certainly manage working with us.

For other systems, this may vary. Some systems who are engineers have different members who may have different technical specialties, or even none at all (a system full of engineers may well have that one member who'd rather draw than code, or similar). Ultimately, if a system is confident enough in themselves to be out under an identity they contribute code under, they likely have methods in place to deal with any problems such as memory gaps or internal disagreements, if they experience those. If you're unsure, a respectful ask will almost certainly be answered.

## How can I be a good ally to plural systems?

We would suggest reading [How Allies Can Support Plurals](https://powertotheplurals.com/how-allies-can-support-plurals/). Beyond that, *talk and listen to us*. Educate others and push back on harmful stereotypes.

Ultimately we can say the same thing here we always say when asked the same for trans people: Act like we're in the room even when we're not. If someone says something that you know would be hurtful, call them out on it, and make your spaces safe ones.

If you run a Discord server, consider adding [PluralKit](https://pluralkit.me) to it.

## I think I might be plural, what should I do?

Talk to us (or other similarly-informed systems) about it, and we'd be happy to give you some pointers. The resources linked in the main page should give you more than enough to go on to find them on your own too. Ultimately nobody can just *tell you* whether you are plural or not, but the community has a lot of experience in helping people through that process. We have spoken to people who were questioning and seen both outcomes, some determining they are and others not. Ultimately, it's your own call if the label fits you - we will never suggest an outcome, just share our observations, what helped us to determine, techniques you can use, and more. We would also suggest [/r/plural](https://reddit.com/r/plural) to speak to systems more generally.

A longstanding classic from the trans community that is easily adapted to many other aspects of personal identity in a similar manner is [The Button Test](https://www.erininthemorning.com/p/the-button-test-how-a-button-press). This is a test you can do on your own, but it may be easier with someone else to talk you through it rather than you playing two separate roles yourself. In particular, questions 5 and 6 in the linked article work perfectly for plurality with nothing more than a `s/transgender/plural`.

Plural-informed therapists and other mental health professionals do exist but are somewhat rare. You are more likely to find one familiar with disordered (DID/OSDD) systems, who may be anywhere from open-minded and helpful, to unhelpful, to actively harmful (e.g. pushing integration as a 'solution') when it comes to non-disordered systems and those with functional multiplicity as a goal. We are the first non-disordered system our current therapist has seen (having seen many DID/OSDD systems), but we trust her because we asked lots of questions about her approach before committing. If you've read this far and are questioning your own plurality, you probably already know the kind of questions you may want to ask.

If you are in significant distress, *safe* crisis resources can be found at https://dontcallthepolice.com - note that mainstream resources may be a lottery if you get someone sympathetic, or someone who makes the situation worse - if those are all that is available, consider working around explicit mentions of plurality. The very worst case imaginable is unfortunately real and has happened before (not just to systems, but to any underprivileged minority such as trans people, disabled people, BIPOC, the unhoused, etc.). This is not us saying to never consider it if you are truly in need, just to be aware of what you are saying and how an uninformed person might react.

## What about if I *want* to be plural, or I feel the *potential* for plurality and want to encourage it?

This is a complicated and sensitive topic that goes far beyond an FAQ entry. If nothing else, do not worry about appropriation - created/intentional systems are valid. If we are to offer any words of caution, it would be that this is very often a lifelong commitment, not something to be taken at all lightly, and can easily result in the creation of a sentient being or beings, someone who can feel the full range of emotions, may have a desire for autonomy and input into your lives, and may not act exactly as you intended them to. Overall, creating a system should receive the same level of consideration as to if it is something you are ready for as having a child would, if not more (babysitters exist, headmatesitters do not). This is not something we can (or *should*) help you directly with past recommended reading, but if you are just looking for permission, this is it.
