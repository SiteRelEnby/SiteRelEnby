# Plurality

*Content Warning: this page discusses formation trauma as a concept, as well as brief mentions of the concepts of medicalisation, pathologisation, fusion, and syscourse-related topics, through an inclusive, pro-endo, and anti-syscourse lens. If none of this means anything to you, it's likely fine, and you should read on.*

Plurality is, in short, the state of having multiple beings sharing the same body. Terminology is never universal, but this state is most commonly referred to as being a **system**, and individual system members may be commonly referred to as **headmates**, although not everyone who is plural identifies with these labels. The opposite of plurality is the state of being a **singlet**, although this itself can still be a spectrum.[^1] This page will not attempt to explain all elements of plurality in depth, or it would become a textbook; it is intended to serve as a starting point for further reading.

Plurality can take any number of different shapes, and different systems can have large differences in areas such as memory[^2], functional differences such as skills that may differ between members, elements of identity, and how (or indeed, if at all) they experience **switching**[^3], who may be regularly **fronting** (see below), and more. Ultimately, the best way to understand how a specific system functions is to listen to their own accounts of things, and respectfully ask questions if needed.

Many (but not all) plural systems formed due to exposure to trauma - this can happen at any time and life stage, not just childhood, and is commonly known as a **traumagenic** system, and contrasted to **endogenic** (a system who has always been plural since their body's birth or otherwise for as long as they can remember, someone who spontaneously became plural without related trauma, or similar origins) and **created** or **intentional** systems (a singlet who intentionally became plural via techniques such as **tulpamancy**, **soulbonding**, or similar). It is possible to identify as multiple origins simultaneously (**mixed-origin**), for example, an originally-endogenic system who subsequently gained new members as a result of trauma may identify as such.[^4]

Many systems, especially those who identify under the plurality umbrella, are happy as systems, and reject the common medical/mass-media narrative that we are 'damaged', 'broken', or "someone to be put back together". As with most things in plurality, this view is not universal, and this often splits along lines of **disordered** and **non-disordered** systems, where a non-disordered system such as ourselves typically does not see plurality as a major problem or impediment to life, and may see it as an extremely important part of their personal identity. This framework is sometimes referred to as **functional multiplicity**, and is where we personally fall as a system. This stands in contrast to disordered systems, who may identify more closely with the medical definitions and labels of **DID and/or OSDD**. Many (but not all) disordered systems see their plurality as an impediment to their functioning to some degree. Singlets who have had exposure to medical models of plurality sometimes see **fusion**, or the merging of members into a singlet, to be a goal for systems - some systems do see this as a goal, but many do not and would rather work towards functional multiplicity. Ultimately, plurality is a way of being like any other, and all systems deserve respect and understanding.

## Start here

- [Plurality - Pluralpedia](https://pluralpedia.org/w/Plurality): Pluralpedia's introduction to plurality. Explains core concepts and gives jumping off points for a deeper dive. Pluralpedia itself is an excellent resource for looking up terminology, but also contains large numbers of microlabels with no notability criteria past "someone decided to create a new term", so is less useful to explore *just* to learn vocabulary.
- [More Than One](https://morethanone.info): Detailed resource covering terminology, system origins, myths, etiquette, and more.
- [Healthy Multiplicity](https://healthymultiplicity.com): Site with in-depth articles on many plurality-related topics.
- [The Plurality Playbook](https://pluralpride.org/playbook): Written by two out plural systems in tech, this is a document first circulated at Google in the early to mid 2010s, including an explanation of plurality, and sections intended for systems, their managers, and their coworkers, and overall represents the most significant writing we are aware of on plural systems working in the tech industry. Note that due to shifts in Google's politics to curry favour with the current US regime, the Google of today is known to be significantly *less* accepting of plural systems than when this guide was first circulated, but it is still an excellent resource that remains unparalleled in its subject coverage, and a reminder of what we hope to one day achieve more widely in tech.

## How this shapes what we build

As mentioned in our main README, we build tools for the plural community to give back, because so many people we are close to are systems, and also because it's an interesting engineering problem. Sheaf is probably the kind of project that gets the bump to staff/principal engineer, if we ever decided to out ourselves to a company we're interviewing for, but Sheaf is by far the most ambitious project we have ever launched and run, and one that we chose to because we feel that we have the skills and attitude necessary to build it, and strengthen the entire plural app development ecosystem at the same time.

Historically, the plural community was concentrated around a few larger apps, in particular, Simply Plural, a system tracker similar in featureset to Sheaf (at this point, a subset of Sheaf's overall features). Simply Plural shut down in June 2026 due to maintainer burnout and scalability problems without the engineers necessary to rearchitect out of them, and this has caused a proliferation of dozens of new plurality-focused apps and tools. Sheaf is one of those, which we started building for much the same reasons, to build a tracker app that we know will scale, do the right thing, and be sustainable in the long term. In addition, as dedicated self-hosters, we wanted to make Sheaf self-hostable - there is a public instance available for free, but we also provide detailed instructions on self-hosting and how to design custom clients. The plural community inherently is one where large amounts of sensitive identity data is needed to be stored, catalogued, and potentially shared in a controlled way, so a commitment to selfhostability, attested and verifiable builds, and transparency were design goals from the very start.

Many of the recent proliferation of plurality-related projects at one point had no direct interoperability past reading in exports from other tools, usually based on reverse-engineering from export files and/or source code, creating a complex web of "what can import what?" where the closest thing to a standard was several apps reimplementing PluralKit's format for their exports with varying degrees of accuracy. [PluralPort](https://pluralport.com) (formerly OpenPlural) is an open and extensible data format first proposed by the developers of [Prism Plural](https://github.com/prismplural) and designed for interoperability so exports from one app can be read directly into another, to make backups and migration between tools less painful. In the time Sheaf has existed, we have already seen several new projects close or go inactive; PluralPort is intended to allow there to always be a migration path between implementing apps, and to lay the groundwork for more complex future interoperability work.
